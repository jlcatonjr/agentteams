Cliff Notes: Overview

An overview of the book, beginning from the Introduction and written to match the text as it describes agentteams 1.1.

The book's argument

The Introduction (Chapter 0) frames the book's problem. Agents that form teams need constitutional scaffolding, and the teams themselves need it as well. Chapter 0 states the task as "to facilitate the development of a team that acts as a self-sustaining and self-authoring system." It argues that the task requires "a theory of governance that scales across levels of analysis, from small teams and families to collections of organizations." Following Coase (1937) and Williamson (1985), it treats an agent team as an organization with an authority structure, a division of labor and a governance regime. What distinguishes a productive team, in Chapter 0's words, "is less a matter of raw model capability than of infrastructure quality". Part VI then turns to the framework that generates such teams, asking where its rules are enforced, how the principal signs exceptions, how a management layer coordinates teams and how an agent repairs code under a signed grant.

Chapter 26 gathers the argument under the heading of designed emergence. The discovery mechanism is engineered, while the content of what it discovers is not, and Chapter 26 puts the point as "The mechanism is designed. The evolving corrective content is emergent." Agent documentation works as cognitive infrastructure in the sense of Caton and Lopez (2020), which Chapter 26 compresses into "The documentation is the institution." The team instantiates the multilevel constitution that Caton (2020b) found in Buchanan, with invariant cores at the constitutional level, agent instructions at the legislative level and individual actions at the operational level. Chapter 26 adds that Chapter 24's level above the teams "is only a relay channel and makes no rules."

Chapter 26 also states the limits plainly. The economic analogy holds at the institutional level but not at the market level, since agents own no property and bear no costs of their own. The evidence comes from a single project. Self-authoring means bounded institutional adaptation, and Chapter 26 states that "The system does not author a new constitution for itself." Where decision signing is enforced, the trust roots give that sentence a mechanism. The routine governance of documentation corrects problems without human intervention, while code repair waits on the principal's grant and merge. Chapter 26 sums up the reflexive loop between theory and artifact as "Theory begat tool. Tool begat team. Team begat text."
